Five Generations, One Record

Family Tree

The documented descendants of Cyrus Holland, drawn from the family's own genealogical register — and the key people and places in Lewes that carried his name forward.

This page follows two family documents: a formal genealogical register, "Descendants of Cyrus Holland," and a one-page family summary, "Key People and Places in the Holland Family History," together with its hand-marked map. Names, dates, and burial locations below are transcribed as the family recorded them. Where a birth or death date wasn't recorded, it's left blank rather than guessed at — and a few entries, especially from the 1800s, may never be fully resolved.

A note on names: family sources differ slightly on Cyrus's wife. Wendell F. Holland's written history names her Cotta Burton, which is the name used throughout this page; the genealogical register records her as Cottie (Cotta) Kegiah. Both point to the same person — the family may want to reconcile which surname is correct.

Key People

Who shaped the story at Oysterrocks

  • 1st Generation Cyrus Holland — the family's story begins with Cyrus. Born into slavery in 1791, he got his freedom on February 9, 1827, and bought the first 40 acres of the family's property at Oyster Rocks some years later.
  • 2nd Generation Eli Holland — took ownership of Oyster Rocks from his father.
  • 3rd Generation, Eli lineage Rev. Jeremiah Holland — distributed the property at Oyster Rocks to his sons, and also owned the property at 313 West 4th Street.
  • 4th Generation, Eli lineage Theodore Winchester Holland, "Uncle/Cousin Winnie" — an entrepreneur who owned a prominent convenience store that was a hub for both Black and white neighbors at the beginning and end of the workday.
  • 4th Generation, Jacob lineage Roy and Edith Holland — entrepreneurs who owned a prominent catering business in Lewes that served key figures in the community, including politicians. The 5th and 6th generations remember being "voluntold" to help — especially picking crab meat for hours for one of the signature dishes, crab balls.

Key Places

Seven sites, marked by the family

  1. Uncle/Cousin Winnie's store — Theodore Winchester Holland's convenience store.
  2. Burton's Chapel and Holland Cemetery — became Holland property by marriage, through Jeremiah (2nd generation).
  3. Oyster Rocks and Holland Family Property — the farm and road that still carry the family name.
  4. Johnnie Walker and Lewes Beach — Johnnie Walker Beach, just east of Lewes Beach, was the designated beach for African American families during segregation.
  5. Shipcarpenter's Square and St. George's AME Church — the church sits on Park Avenue, across from Shipcarpenter's Square.
  6. "Grandmom's" House — 313 West 4th Street.
  7. Black Cemetery in uptown Lewes

Descendants of Cyrus Holland

Generation 1

Cyrus Holland and his wife, and the children who carried the Holland name into the next generation.

Cyrus Holland
b. 1791, Delaware — d. 1881

Born into slavery; freed February 9, 1827 by Elizabeth Holland. Married Cotta Burton (b. 1800, Sussex County, Delaware — d. 1870). Bought his first 40 acres at Oyster Rocks in December 1845.

Children of Cyrus Holland and Cotta Burton:

  • Jacob W. Holland (1823–1884) — m. Cecelia Gumby
  • James Holland (b. 1827)
  • Hannah Holland (b. 1832)
  • Jeremiah Holland (1834–1913, Burtons Chapel AME Church Cemetery) — m. Ruth Emily Burton, 1858
  • Mary Holland (b. 1837, Sussex County, DE)
  • Ellenor Holland (b. 1840)
  • Ann E. Holland (1842–1915)
  • James H. Holland (b. 1845)
  • Elzey Eli Holland (b. 1829) — m. Elizabeth Holland
